\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The separator certificate is one three-move loop, applied twice:
\emph{rewrite exactly} (law of total covariance), \emph{bound only the
remainder} (Cauchy--Schwarz), \emph{get every standard deviation by
subtraction} (law of total variance).  The first pass conditions on the
whole exterior and already yields a certificate; the second conditions
on the hubs and yields the one the build uses.  Three ledgers stay
explicit throughout: which $j$'s are computed and which are capped;
where exactness ends; where each quantity comes from ($Q$ side,
$\Sigma$ side, subtraction).
\end{abstract}

\section{Two zones}\label{sec:zones}

A zero-mean Gaussian field $x \sim N(0, \Sigma)$, $Q = \Sigma^{-1}$
sparse; data point $j$ carries $\eta_j = a_j\tr x$ with sparse support.
The \emph{group} of a target $i$ is the top level sets of
$|\Cor(\eta_i,\eta_j)|$ over all $j$; $v$ is the deepest accepted
absolute correlation ($v_{\mathrm{last}}$ in the build).

The build walks outward from $\supp(a_i)$ on the graph of $Q$, avoiding
the hubs $H$ (few global latents: intercepts, small components),
collecting a \textbf{core} $K$; the \textbf{proposal} is
$\operatorname{lift}(K)$, the data points whose support touches $K$.
Two zones, treated in opposite ways:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Inside the proposal:} finitely many candidates, each
  correlation computed exactly (sparse solves, no bounding).  The group
  and $v$ come from here.
\item \textbf{Outside} (the \emph{far field}:
  $\supp(a_j) \cap K = \varnothing$, i.e.\ supported in $F := K^c$):
  never enumerated.  The certificate is one $j$-free number
  $\mathrm{bnd}_i$ with $|\Cor(\eta_i,\eta_j)| \le \mathrm{bnd}_i$ for
  every far-field $j$; $\mathrm{bnd}_i < v$ certifies the group
  complete.
\end{itemize}

Exactness is priced per $j$, and the far field is almost everybody.
Hence the rule the rest of these notes keeps explicit: \emph{everything
$j$-dependent is computed inside the proposal, or capped $j$-free
outside it.}

\section{The loop}\label{sec:loop}

Normalize every variable to unit variance; covariances \emph{are}
correlations.  One iteration:

\paragraph{Move 1 — rewrite exactly.}  Pick a conditioning set $x_A$;
the law of total covariance gives
\[
  \Cov(\eta_i, \eta_j)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Cov\bigl(\E[\eta_i \mid x_A],\, \E[\eta_j \mid
  x_A]\bigr)}_{\text{ledger term}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\E\bigl[\Cov(\eta_i, \eta_j \mid x_A)\bigr]}_{\text{remainder}} .
\]
An identity: signed, nothing lost.  A good $A$ makes one term
tractable --- the remainder dies, or the ledger term becomes computable
structure.

\paragraph{Move 2 — bound only the remainder.}  Cauchy--Schwarz,
$|\Cov(U,V)| \le \sd(U)\,\sd(V)$, on the uncomputed term --- never on
the whole covariance.  Every $j$-dependent factor then takes a $j$-free
cap: a supremum computed once, or $\sd \le 1$.

\paragraph{Move 3 — sd by subtraction.}  The variables of Move 2 are
global objects one cannot assemble; their \emph{variances} survive,
by the law of total variance:
\[
  \Var\bigl(\E[\eta \mid x_A]\bigr)
  \;=\; \Var(\eta) \;-\; \E\bigl[\Var(\eta \mid x_A)\bigr]
  \;=\; \text{total} \;-\; \text{the computable part.}
\]
For a GMRF the subtracted part is local: conditional on everything
outside a set, the conditional precision is a subblock of $Q$ --- one
small Cholesky.  Compute what can be computed; subtract it from the
total.

\section{Level 0: conditioning on the whole exterior}\label{sec:level0}

\paragraph{Move 1.}  Condition on $x_F$.  A far-field $\eta_j$ is a
function of $x_F$: the remainder vanishes and
$\E[\eta_j\mid x_F] = \eta_j$, so
\begin{equation}
  \Cov(\eta_i,\eta_j) \;=\; \Cov\bigl(\E[\eta_i\mid x_F],\; \eta_j\bigr)
  \qquad\text{exactly.}
  \label{eq:lvl0rewrite}
\end{equation}
Only the part of the target \emph{visible to the exterior} carries
covariance out; the private fluctuation
$\eta_i - \E[\eta_i\mid x_F]$ left the ledger by an identity, not an
estimate.

\paragraph{Move 2.}
$|\Cov(\eta_i,\eta_j)| \le \sd\bigl(\E[\eta_i\mid x_F]\bigr)\cdot 1$.

\paragraph{Move 3.}
\[
  \Var\bigl(\E[\eta_i\mid x_F]\bigr)
  = 1 - \sigma_i^2,
  \qquad
  \sigma_i^2 = \E\Var(\eta_i\mid x_F) = a_K\tr\, Q_{KK}^{-1}\, a_K :
\]
given $x_F$ only $x_K$ is random, with conditional precision $Q_{KK}$.

\begin{theorem}[Level-0 certificate]\label{thm:lvl0}
For every far-field $\eta_j$,
$\;|\Cor(\eta_i,\eta_j)| \le \sqrt{1 - \sigma_i^2}$.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Why it is not enough.}  The visible variance contains
everything the hubs explain: $1 - \sigma_i^2 \ge \rho_i^2$ (the
hub-explained share), a floor at $\rho_i$.  Correlations through a
bottleneck \emph{multiply}: with hub share $t$ on both sides, true
far-field correlations --- and $v$ with them --- plateau near $t$,
while the bound plateaus near $\sqrt t$.  The bound is the square root
of the thing it must beat; nothing certifies.

\section{Level 1: conditioning on the hubs}\label{sec:level1}

Run the loop again on the surviving pair of \eqref{eq:lvl0rewrite},
with conditioning set $x_H$.

\paragraph{Move 1.}  Total covariance plus the tower rule
$\E\bigl[\E[\eta_i\mid x_F]\,\big|\,x_H\bigr] = \E[\eta_i\mid x_H]$
give
\begin{equation}
  \Cov(\eta_i,\eta_j)
  \;=\;
  \underbrace{\Cov(h_i,\, h_j)}_{\text{hub channel (ledger)}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{\Cov(l_i,\, e_j)}_{\text{leak channel (remainder)}}
  \qquad\text{exactly,}
  \label{eq:lvl1rewrite}
\end{equation}
with $h = \E[\eta\mid x_H]$,
$l_i = \E[\eta_i\mid x_F] - \E[\eta_i\mid x_H]$,
$e_j = \eta_j - h_j$.  The division of labour has flipped: at level~0
the remainder died and the ledger carried everything; here the ledger
term is computable structure ($|H|$-dimensional regressions) and the
remainder is what Cauchy--Schwarz gets.  Each pass peels one more
computable layer off the unknown.

\paragraph{Move 2.}  Both channels are capped --- the far field is
never touched, so nothing stays unbounded:
\[
  |\Cov(h_i,h_j)| \;\le\; \rho_i\,\rho_j \;\le\; \rho_i\,\rho_{\max},
  \qquad
  |\Cov(l_i,e_j)| \;\le\; \sd(l_i)\,\sd(e_j) \;\le\; \sd(l_i)\cdot 1 .
\]
Provenance of $\rho_{\max}$: the maximum of $\rho_j$ over \emph{all}
data points.  It is free --- every $j$ is the target of its own build,
so the hub shares exist for the whole field anyway ($|H|$ global solves
for the hub columns, then one $|H|$-dimensional quadratic form per
point) --- and it is global rather than per-far-field because the far
field varies with $i$; one maximum dominates them all.  The price is
conservatism: the maximizer may sit inside $i$'s own proposal.  (The
per-$j$ prohibition concerns per-\emph{pair} work; a once-per-$j$
vector amortized over all targets is exempt.)

\paragraph{Move 3.}  Two subtractions in cascade:
\[
  \Var(l_i)
  \;=\; \underbrace{\bigl(1 - \sigma_i^2\bigr)}_{\text{level 0: total
  minus private}}
  \;-\; \underbrace{\rho_i^2}_{\text{level 1: minus the hubs}},
  \qquad
  \rho_i^2 = c_H\tr \Sigma_{HH}^{-1} c_H,\;\; c_H = (\Sigma a_i)_H ,
\]
the latter on the $\Sigma$ side from the shared hub columns.  Each
level subtracts the layer it just made computable; the rest is
surrendered to Cauchy--Schwarz.

\begin{theorem}[Level-1 certificate, as used]\label{thm:lvl1}
For every far-field $\eta_j$,
\[
  |\Cor(\eta_i,\eta_j)|
  \;\le\;
  \mathrm{bnd}_i
  \;:=\;
  \rho_i\,\rho_{\max}
  \;+\;
  \sqrt{\,1 - \sigma_i^2 - \rho_i^2\,}\;.
\]
$\mathrm{bnd}_i < v$ (strictly, outside the tie band) certifies the
group complete.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[$R_i^2$ is a repackaging]
The companion notes write the leak term as
$\sqrt{1-\rho_i^2}\,\sqrt{R_i^2}$,
$R_i^2 = 1 - \sigma_i^2/(1-\rho_i^2)$.  Here it appears directly as the
double subtraction $\sqrt{1 - \sigma_i^2 - \rho_i^2}$; $R_i^2$ is the
same number normalized by the hub residual, and need never be
introduced.
\end{remark}

\section{Why splitting before bounding never loses}\label{sec:split}

Both levels are pure bounding; what did the second pass buy?  A
theorem:

\begin{lemma}[Channelwise beats lumped]\label{lem:split}
For each far-field $j$,
\[
  \rho_i\rho_j + \sd(l_i)\,\sd(e_j)
  \;\le\;
  \sqrt{\rho_i^2 + \Var(l_i)}\;\sqrt{\rho_j^2 + \Var(e_j)}
  \;=\; \sqrt{1-\sigma_i^2}\cdot 1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Cauchy--Schwarz in $\mathbb R^2$ on $(\rho_i, \sd(l_i))$ and
$(\rho_j, \sd(e_j))$; the right side evaluates by
$\rho_i^2 + \Var(l_i) = 1 - \sigma_i^2$ and
$\rho_j^2 + \Var(e_j) = 1$.
\end{proof}

Two readings of the gain.  \emph{Weaker worst case:} lumped
Cauchy--Schwarz charges for ``the whole visible part aligns with
$\eta_j$''; channelwise only for ``hub aligns with hub, leak with
residual''.  The finer the rewrite, the less alignment information each
bound discards.  \emph{The product cap:} after splitting, the hub cap
is $\rho_i\rho_{\max}$, inheriting the multiply-through-a-bottleneck
structure; lumped bounding buries $\rho_i^2$ under the root and floors
at $\rho_i$.

One caveat: after uniformization the comparison stops being a theorem.
At $\rho_{\max}$ near $1$ the level-1 cap
$\rho_i\rho_{\max} + \sd(l_i)$ can exceed $\sqrt{1-\sigma_i^2}$ (at
$\rho_{\max}=1$ it always does, slightly).  That regime is the failure
mode ``$H$ too greedy'', where nothing certifies anyway; and both caps
being valid, their minimum is free.  (The build uses the level-1 cap
alone.)

Everything above the Cauchy--Schwarz row is signed and exact;
opposite-signed channels cost tightness, never validity.

\section{Cost, failure, level 2}\label{sec:cost}

\paragraph{Per target.}  One Cholesky of $Q_{KK}$ ($\sigma_i^2$, $Q$
side); one $|H|$-dimensional quadratic form ($\rho_i^2$, $\Sigma$ side,
amortized); one subtraction; one comparison with $v$.  Per far-field
$j$: nothing.

\paragraph{Failure is a size, not an error.}
$\mathrm{bnd}_i \ge v$ means $K$ is too small.  Growing $K$ feeds
$\sigma_i^2$ and starves $\sqrt{1-\sigma_i^2-\rho_i^2}$ monotonically
--- the mathematics of the grow-retry rounds.  Smooth fields, or $v$ at
the noise floor, refuse and fall back to exact solves: conservative,
never wrong.

\paragraph{Level 2.}  The loop does not care what the conditioning
variables are.  Hyperparameters $\theta$ in the slot
($\Cov = \E_\theta\Cov_\theta + \Cov$ of the per-$\theta$ means) would
lift the certificate to mixture-level correlations; hard linear
constraints already ride the slot as a low-rank correction.  One more
global mechanism, one more pass of \emph{rewrite, bound, subtract}.
The loop is the theory.
